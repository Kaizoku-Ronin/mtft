Sphereland

The visual companion to the MTFT repository: twenty-five instruments, each a small exact computation you can steer. It began as Flatland built for physics (pick the sphere you are, the space you live in, the sphere you wrap) and grew into a tour of knots one dimension up, mass gaps, and the modular torus.

Reading mode

Every panel says which layer it belongs to: a mathematical structure, a model you have chosen, what follows from the model, or an MTFT reading. The readings are hypotheses, folded away until you open them. Essentials shows the controls, the figures and their captions; everything adds the fact lists, the notes and every number behind them. Values are computed in the page or certified by the Python scripts in checks/, each of which ends with ALL CHECKS PASSED.

INSTRUMENT 2 · WHAT YOU CAN WRAP structure

The sphere table πn(Sm)

Rows: you, Sn. Columns: the sphere you wrap, Sm. Each entry is the group of ways a map Sn → Sm can wrap (for n = 0 a two-element set, not a group). It becomes a physical charge only once a field is specified that takes values in Sm on the sphere you are, with dynamics that preserve the class. Diagonal: the degree. Above it, zero. Below it, the strange numbers.

INSTRUMENT 3 · THE HOLES CHART structure

Remove an object; what shape is the hole?

Read down for the object removed and across for the space it runs through. Remove a flat p-plane from Rd and what is left retracts onto Sd−p−1: S⁰ means the space splits in two, S¹ a loop that cannot be shrunk. It is Instrument 1 read backwards, because the sphere that detects a hole is the shape of the hole itself: n + p = d − 1. A curved or knotted object has the same homology around it (Alexander duality) but can have a different homotopy type; Instrument 8 shows what the knotting looks like.

Hold the geometry, change the dynamics: inertia, stiffness, damping

INSTRUMENT 8 · THE KNOT MOVIE structure

Knotting one dimension up, seen as a movie one dimension down

A knotted object in d dimensions is seen by a (d − 1)-dimensional observer as a sequence of slices: a movie whose frames are one dimension lower. Take a knotted arc K in 3-space with its ends on the floor. A Flatlander slicing it at height c sees only points, and the knotting is in how the points dance as c runs. Spin the same arc about the floor and it sweeps out a knotted sphere in 4-space, Artin's spun knot; a Spacelander slicing that at height c sees the part of K above c together with its mirror image, a closed curve whose knot type changes as c passes the turns of the arc. The spun hoop is the ordinary sphere of Instrument 1.

INSTRUMENT 9 · THE SLICE CURVE structurederivedhypothesis

The modular parameter of a slice

The 2n points where the arc meets the plane are branch points: the double cover of the plane branched at them is a hyperelliptic curve of genus n − 1 (Riemann–Hurwitz, χ = 4 − 2n = 2 − 2g). Four points, a 2-bridge presentation, give an elliptic curve whose period τ lives in the upper half-plane modulo SL(2,ℤ), the modular curve's own ground; six points give genus 2 and a period matrix in Siegel's H₂ modulo Sp(4,ℤ). As c sweeps, the slice curve moves through its moduli space and degenerates exactly at the Morse levels, where branch points collide and the discriminant vanishes. What the knot owns is not a point but the path's class together with the pairing of the points at its ends: Birman's plat closure, with Hilden's subgroup absorbing the choices. Birman–Hilden sends braids of 2g + 2 points to the hyperelliptic mapping class group and on to Sp(2g, ℤ); for g = 1 that is the modular group itself, and one half-twist is one Dehn twist T.

INSTRUMENT 10 · THE TREFOIL COMPLEMENT structurederivedhypothesis

The modular group's unit tangent bundle is the trefoil complement

A lattice in the plane with unit covolume is a point of SL(2,ℝ)/SL(2,ℤ), which is also the unit tangent bundle of the modular surface: the lattice's shape is τ, its rotation is the direction of a tangent vector. Weierstrass turns the lattice into the pair (g₂, g₃), with g₂³ − 27g₃² ≠ 0; normalising along the scaling (t⁴g₂, t⁶g₃) lands on S³, and the discriminant locus g₂³ = 27g₃², where lattices degenerate, is a trefoil knot on it. So (Milnor) the space of lattices is S³ minus a trefoil. The circle of rotations over each τ is a (2,3) curve parallel to the trefoil, except over τ = i and τ = ρ, where the lattice's own symmetry makes the circle close early: the two exceptional fibres, of orders 2 and 3, which form a Hopf link. A closed geodesic on the modular surface, lifted to its tangent vectors, is a closed curve here, a modular knot kA for the hyperbolic matrix A that closes it. Ghys: the modular knots are exactly the Lorenz knots, and the linking number of kA with the trefoil is Rademacher's function ψ(A), which Atiyah showed is also the η-invariant, the signature defect, Meyer's function, and the monodromy of log Δ. On the word A = La₁Rb₁⋯ it is simply ψ = Σb − Σa.

INSTRUMENT 11 · THE QUBIT structuremodelderived

The Hopf fibration is a qubit

The normalised states of a qubit, (z₀, z₁) with |z₀|² + |z₁|² = 1, form S³; the state up to its global phase is a point of the Bloch sphere S² = ℂP¹; the map between them is the Hopf fibration, and the fibre over each Bloch point is the circle of phases. "Preimage continuity" is the local triviality of this bundle. Its global twist is that the fibres over any two distinct Bloch points are linked once: the Hopf invariant of cell (3,2) in the sphere table, which is also the Chern number of the bundle, the charge of the Dirac monopole it carries, and the reason a spin-½ taken around a loop on the Bloch sphere comes back with the Berry phase −Ω/2, half the solid angle enclosed. Below, two Bloch points and their linked fibres, and the horizontal lift of a latitude loop, which fails to close by exactly that phase. Then two qubits: S⁷ ⊂ ℂ⁴ ≅ ℍ², where the quaternionic Hopf map S⁷ → S⁴ is entanglement-sensitive (Mosseri–Dandoloff 2001).

Two qubits: the quaternionic Hopf map S³ → S⁷ → S⁴

Write |Ψ⟩ = α|00⟩ + β|01⟩ + γ|10⟩ + δ|11⟩ as the quaternion pair q₁ = α + βj, q₂ = γ + δj. The Hopf map (q₁, q₂) → (2q₁q̄₂, |q₁|² − |q₂|²) lands on S⁴ ⊂ ℝ⁵, and its coordinates are physics: the complex and real parts are the reduced Bloch vector r of the first qubit, and the j-part is −2 det[α β; γ δ], whose modulus is the concurrence C = 2|αδ − βγ|. Since |r|² + C² = 1, every two-qubit state sits on a quarter circle between the separable S² (C = 0, qubit 1 pure) and the entangled circle (r = 0, qubit 1 maximally mixed). The fibre S³ is the local SU(2) acting on the second qubit; SU(2) on the first qubit rotates r on its sphere; the global phase turns the j-part. All three leave C alone, which is why C measures entanglement.

INSTRUMENT 12 · THE MASS GAP structuremodelderivedhypothesis

The gap: no scale classically, a scale from the running, a theorem at strong coupling

Classical Yang–Mills in four dimensions has no parameter with dimensions: the action (1/2g²)∫Tr F∧⋆F is invariant under dilations, so nothing in the classical theory can be a mass, and a static lump of field in three space dimensions has energy E[Aλ] = λ4−dE[A] = λE[A] under x → x/λ (Derrick): spread it and its energy goes to zero, there is no threshold. The quantum theory makes a scale out of the coupling's running: with b₀ = 11N/3 > 0, Λ = μ·(b₀g²/16π²)−b₁/2b₀²e−8π²/b₀g² is the same whatever μ you compute it at, and a gap, if there is one, is a pure number times Λ. On the lattice the gap is a theorem at strong coupling (Osterwalder–Seiler 1978): a reflection-positive action has a positive transfer matrix, and the cluster expansion gives m(0⁺⁺)a = −4 ln uf and an area law σa² = −ln uf at leading order, with uf the fundamental character coefficient of the Boltzmann weight. What is open, for every action, is a gap that survives a → 0. The Clay problem asks for exactly that, on ℝ⁴, for every compact simple group.

INSTRUMENT 13 · KAKEYA structure

A needle in every direction, in almost no room

Kakeya (1917) asked for the least area in which a unit needle can be turned through every direction. Besicovitch (1928) answered: as little as you like, and even a set of measure zero can contain a unit segment in every direction. The construction below is his Perron tree: a triangle cut into 2k thin triangles with a common apex, the pieces slid along the base so that their "hearts" pile up level by level; every piece still contains a unit segment in each of its directions, and the union's area is at most [α2k + 2(1−α)²(1−α2k)/(1−α²)]·area(T), which goes to zero as α → 1 and k → ∞ (slowly). What such a set cannot do is be small in dimension: the Kakeya conjecture says a set with a unit segment in every direction of ℝn has Hausdorff dimension n. Proven for n = 2 (Davies 1971) and for n = 3 (Wang and Zahl, 2025, after a century); open for every n ≥ 4. Over finite fields it is a theorem in every dimension (Dvir 2008): a set in 𝔽qn containing a line in every direction has at least C(q+n−1, n) ≥ qn/n! points, proved in two pages with a polynomial, which is the opposite of a measure-theoretic argument.

INSTRUMENT 14 · THE PERIOD CLOCK structurehypothesis

A base is a clock, a modulus is a system, the period is the return time

Write n/p in base B. The digits repeat with period k = ordp(B), the number of ticks before Bk ≡ 1 (mod p); the remainders go round a cycle of length k inside (ℤ/p)×, and the other cycles are its cosets, φ(p)/k loops in all. Periods are cyclotomic: ordp(B) = k exactly when p divides Φk(B), so the primes with decimal period 6 are the prime factors of Φ₆(10) = 91 = 7 × 13, and no others. When k is even, Bk/2 ≡ −1 and the two halves of the repetend are complements summing to Bk/2 − 1 (Midy). Here is where the knots come in: Φk(t) is the Alexander polynomial of the torus knot T(a, b) whenever k = ab with a, b ≥ 2 coprime, and the monodromy of that knot's fibration has order exactly k. For k = 6 it is the trefoil: Δ = t² − t + 1 is the characteristic polynomial of the order-6 element ST of SL(2,ℤ), the trefoil's monodromy, and ST³ = −1 is Midy's complement. The trefoil's Alexander module is the Eisenstein ring ℤ[ζ₆]: 7 and 13 split in it (13 = 4² − 4·1 + 1²), which is why 10 can be a sixth root of unity mod 13; 11 is inert. Over 𝔽₁₃ with t = 10 the trefoil has 13² Alexander-quandle colourings instead of the trivial 13: the decimal period of 13 is a representation of the trefoil group.

INSTRUMENT 15 · BESSEL KNOTS structurederivedhypothesis

A knot tied in nothing: the nodal line of four Bessel beams, and the zeta function it carries

Solutions of the Helmholtz equation (∇² + 1)ψ = 0 separate in cylindrical coordinates into Bessel beams Jm(bR) eimφ eiz√(1−b²), and in ℝd the radial solutions are r1−d/2Jℓ+d/2−1(r): "the Bessel solution space" is the Helmholtz solution space, in every dimension. Berry and Dennis (2001) tune three Jm beams, ψ₀ = eimφF(R, z), so that the ring where F vanishes is doubly degenerate — F = FR = Fz = 0 at R*, and the equation itself then forces FRR = −Fzz, which is the knotting kind of degeneracy — and add a weak J₀ beam. The degenerate ring unfolds into the (m, 2) torus knot, the trefoil for m = 3, threaded by an m-stranded helix where the axial vortex of strength m unfolds too. Below, the nodal line is tracked in the actual field, and the project script computes its Alexander polynomial from a projection of the tracked curve: t² − t + 1. Nothing is modelled here; it is the exact statement that a knot is something the Bessel solution space can do, in a free field, at no cost. Enciso and Peralta-Salas (2013) make it a theorem in every dimension: any compact codimension-2 submanifold with trivial normal bundle — every n-knot in ℝn+2 — is, after a small diffeomorphism, a component of {u₁ = u₂ = 0} for two solutions of any second-order elliptic equation with analytic coefficients, Helmholtz included.

INSTRUMENT 16 · THE GAP UNDER REFINEMENT structuremodelhypothesis

Which gaps survive refinement, and who pays for them

"A gap uniform in the lattice spacing" means: refine the lattice, retune the coupling along the renormalisation trajectory, and the lowest excitation above the vacuum stays a fixed positive number in physical units. Here are the refinements where that is a theorem, the ones where it is false, and the one where it is the whole problem. The first chart is the clean case: four families of graphs that grow, and the spectral gap of each — the clock ℤ/n (Instrument 14's period cycles), the flat torus (ℤ/L)³ (a free massless field: no gap in infinite volume), the modular group's finite quotients PSL(2, 𝔽p) on the generators S, T (an expander family by Selberg's theorem — property τ — though with a small guaranteed constant that these sizes do not reach), and the Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak graphs X5,q, built from the six four-square representations of 5, which are Ramanujan: every non-trivial eigenvalue has |λ| ≤ 2√5, so the gap is at least 6 − 2√5 for every q — the Riemann hypothesis for the Ihara zeta of Instrument 15, and the optimum (Alon–Boppana). That is the one place in mathematics where arithmetic provably supplies a gap uniform in a refinement, and it is paid for by the quaternions: r₄(p) = 8(p + 1) lattice points on the shell of radius² p in ℤ⁴ give the p + 1 generators. The second chart is two-dimensional gauge theory, where uniformity in a is exact (Migdal) for the Wilson action and for CC-69's repaired action alike; the third is four dimensions, where the strong-coupling gap and asymptotic scaling refuse to agree, which is the open conjecture stated as a picture.

INSTRUMENT 18 · GRAVITY'S KNOT structuremodelhypothesis

The dimensional formula of G is Kepler's cusp, and the cusp's link is the trefoil

[G] = M⁻¹L³T⁻² says three things: a mass in the denominator (gravity couples to mass), L³ from Gauss's law in three space dimensions (flux through a sphere grows as r²), and T⁻² from acceleration. Put a mass M at the origin and the L³T⁻² becomes Kepler's third law, a³ = (GM/4π²)T² — in d spacetime dimensions [G] = M⁻¹Ld−1T⁻² and rd−1 = cT². That curve, y² = cxd−1, is a cusp, and the link of a cusp — its intersection with a small sphere in ℂ² — is the torus link T(2, d−1): the Hopf link in 2+1 (where gravity is Chern–Simons theory and its observable is linking), the trefoil in our 3+1, the cinquefoil in 5+1. The value of G rescales the cusp without changing its knot; what G sets is the scale of the picture. Three more exact things follow and are drawn below: the Kepler problem is the shadow under the Hopf map of a four-dimensional harmonic oscillator (Kustaanheimo–Stiefel); orbital resonances are torus knots on the longitude torus, Neptune–Pluto's 3:2 a trefoil; and in 2+1 dimensions G is literally the knot parameter — the Chern–Simons level k = ℓ/4G at which Wilson loops are Jones polynomials — while the modular knots of Instrument 10 are the horizons of BTZ black holes whose mass and entropy G converts from their lengths. Last, the programme's own G (Paper 31) is the modulus of the Dedekind eta function at the confinement depth — the same function whose phase round a modular knot is the linking number of Instrument 17 — shown with its sensitivity, as a model.

INSTRUMENT 20 · POISSON'S RATIO structuremodelhypothesis

Poisson's ratio is the weight of the generalized Kronecker delta: it never makes a mass scale, it fixes mass ratios, mass signs, and which modes exist

Hooke's law for an isotropic body is built from Kronecker deltas, and only two combinations survive: Cijkl = λδijδkl + μ(δikδjl + δilδjk). Poisson's ratio is their ratio, and it is a pure number — so it cannot set a mass by itself. A mass scale always comes from a length (a thickness, a torus, a core radius) or a stiffness. What ν sets is everything else. The exact form of that statement is a rearrangement: every isotropic energy is W = ½M(tr ε)² − 2μσ₂(ε), where M = λ + 2μ is the P-wave modulus and σ₂ is the sum of the 2×2 minors, i.e. the generalized Kronecker delta δijkl contracted with the strain twice. ν is the share between the two terms, 2μ/M = (1 − 2ν)/(1 − ν). The σ₂ term recurs throughout this lab. On a surface it is Gauss's curvature, so its integral is 4π times the degree of the Gauss map. On gradient fields it is a total divergence. In space it is the kinetic term of general relativity, and in spacetime it is the only consistent graviton mass term. The medium made of σ₂ alone (M = 0) is the "labile" aether of Cauchy (1839) and Kelvin (1888). INSTRUMENT 21 · THE AETHER structurederivedmodelhypothesis

The aether, made exact: zero stiffness freezes a mode but only a constraint removes it; an elastic torus is a modular medium whose energy is an Eisenstein series; the inverse-square law is that torus seen from far away

For fifty years physics treated light as a wave in an elastic solid and tried to get rid of that solid's third wave, the longitudinal one. Green (1837) made the solid incompressible, so the third wave became infinitely fast. Cauchy (1839) and Kelvin (1888) set its longitudinal stiffness to zero, so the wave stood still. MacCullagh (1839) gave the medium energy only for rotation, and his equations are Maxwell's. Each of these aethers is one value of a single number, r = cL²/cT² = (λ + 2μ)/μ, and this instrument puts them all on one slider. It settles the classic objection exactly: a zero stiffness only freezes the longitudinal mode, and only a constraint removes it — a Lagrange multiplier such as Maxwell's A₀ or Green's pressure. It then takes an elastic torus seriously as a medium for modular time. Its energy has to be a function on the modular curve. For power-law forces that function is Instrument 19's Eisenstein series, for logarithmic (vortex) forces it is Kronecker's, and for the vacuum of a six-dimensional world it is E(τ, 3). Its lowest point is the hexagonal torus, whose stiffness is fixed by a Laplace eigenvalue. Four classical pictures supply the remaining sections: the phase-portrait chart is the anatomy of a deformation, kissing numbers count a torus's lightest states, the inverse-square law is a torus seen from far away, and the matharium coil is Kepler's equation.

INSTRUMENT 22 · THE MODULAR FLIP structurederivedmodelhypothesis

The modular flip: one torus, two ways to fill it. Cap the space circle and the world is confined and gapped; cap the time circle and it is a fluid. The shortest cycle always caps, so the phase diagram is the Farey tiling

A field theory at temperature T on a spatial circle of length L lives, in Euclidean time, on a torus with two circles: β = 1/T and L. Its shape is τ = θ + iβ/L, where the twist θ is how far the time circle is slid along the space circle. In holography that torus is the boundary of a smooth higher-dimensional space, and the simplest bulk spaces are solid tori: one of the two circles is filled in by a disc and shrinks smoothly to a point inside. If the space circle shrinks, the geometry ends in a smooth tip. Its spectrum is discrete and gapped, its particles are glueballs of mass about 1/L, and a string wrapped on the time circle cannot be removed, so the Polyakov loop vanishes: the theory is confined. This is Witten's 1998 model of confinement, the AdS soliton of Horowitz and Myers. If the time circle shrinks, there is a horizon and entropy of order N², and the long-wavelength dynamics is a fluid with η/s = 1/4π: the theory is deconfined. The two are the same smooth manifold with the circles relabelled, and the relabelling β ↔ L is τ → −1/τ, the S of the modular group. Which filling wins is decided by one rule, in every dimension: the cycle that caps is the shortest one. INSTRUMENT 23 · TWO STRIPS structurederivedmodelhypothesis

Two strips: a mass gap is a strip of analyticity in momentum, smoothness is a strip of analyticity in position. Burgers' equation shows the second strip closing in finite time without viscosity, and held open with it

The two fillings of Instrument 22 face two different Millennium problems, and the problems ask for the same kind of object. A mass gap m means correlations die off like e−m|x|. By the Paley–Wiener theorem that is the same as saying the Fourier transform continues analytically into the strip |Im p| < m, and the Källén–Lehmann representation puts its first singularity at p = ±im exactly. A smooth flow is one whose Fourier coefficients die off like e−δ|k|, which is the same as saying the velocity continues analytically into the strip |Im x| < δ. Foias and Temam (1989) showed that a Navier–Stokes solution is analytic in such a strip for as long as it stays smooth, so a blowup at time T* is exactly δ(t) → 0 as t → T*. It is one theorem, read in two variables. Burgers' equation, Navier–Stokes in one dimension with the pressure removed, can be solved exactly both ways and shows both behaviours. Without viscosity a complex singularity marches onto the real line and the strip closes at the shock; with viscosity the singularities are poles that never arrive.

INSTRUMENT 24 · THE GAP ON THE MODULAR CURVE structurederivedhypothesis

The gap on the modular curve: the hyperbolic plane has a built-in gap of ¼, Selberg conjectured that arithmetic keeps it, and at level 143 it is a theorem. The modular surface's scattering phase has the Riemann zeros as resonances, all of one width exactly when RH holds

Instrument 22 drew the shape space of a torus, the upper half-plane modulo SL(2,ℤ); this instrument listens to it. The hyperbolic plane has a built-in mass gap. Its Laplacian satisfies −Δys = s(1 − s)ys, waves live on the line s = ½ + ir, and there s(1 − s) = ¼ + r² ≥ ¼. Every quotient of finite area keeps that continuum, carried by the Eisenstein series E(τ, ½ + ir) of Instruments 19 and 21, and adds the constant function at 0 and possibly further eigenvalues. Selberg conjectured in 1965 that for the congruence quotients, the arithmetic ones (X₀(N) among them), nothing falls in between: λ₁ ≥ ¼. It is the archimedean case of the Ramanujan conjecture. It is proven at level 143, and it is false for surfaces in general: pinch a neck and λ₁ → 0. The same surface carries a second spectrum, of resonances. Its scattering phase is built from ζ(2s) and has a pole at s = ρ/2 for every Riemann zero ρ. These sit at Re s = ¼ exactly when the zeros sit at Re s = ½, so the Riemann hypothesis says that every resonance of the modular surface decays at the same rate.
