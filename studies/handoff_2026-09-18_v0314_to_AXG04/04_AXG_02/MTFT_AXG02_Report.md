# MTFT AXG-02: the six-dimensional parent, flux-compatible axions, and a scalar vacuum test

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Input:** supplied MTFT 0.31.4 candidate, M1 stack data; AXG-01 conventions  
**Classification:** exact conditional algebra and topological constraints; no completed parent theory or physical vacuum

## Abstract

AXG-01 established a local four-dimensional anomaly-cancellation construction. AXG-02 asks whether its fields can be lifted to six dimensions and whether a minimal neutral-scalar extension can stabilize the internal radius. The simplest explicit six-dimensional fermion lift has a mixed Abelian–color anomaly and an irreducible gravitational anomaly, even though its flux reduction reproduces AXG-01 exactly. Ordinary two-form Green–Schwarz terms cannot cancel those two contributions alone.

A change of six-dimensional chirality in a zero-index sector aligns the mixed color anomaly with the existing charge combination $k_2-k_1$, leaving all four-dimensional chiral indices unchanged. Independently, smooth native six-dimensional Stückelberg scalars must have shift charges annihilating the internal flux. Of the two-dimensional AXG-01 shift span, precisely the line generated by $k_2-k_1$ passes that necessary test. This identifies a candidate with different origins for the two axions, not a completed anomaly cancellation.

For the vacuum question, dimensional reduction gives a restricted two-field potential. Nonnegative bulk scalar potential leaves the radius runaway. A negative exponential term can give a two-field AdS minimum only under a definite exponent condition derived below. Neither the negative term nor its exponents are determined by the anomaly data.

## 3. The anomaly polynomial and its exact reduction

### 3.1 Conventions and a hand-replication formula

Let $f_i=F_i/(2\pi)$ be formal normalized Abelian curvature two-forms. Write $c_{2,c},c_{3,c}$ for the $SU(3)$ Chern classes, $c_{2,L}$ for the $SU(2)$ second Chern class, and $p_1,p_2$ for tangent-bundle Pontryagin classes. For the chosen complex Weyl chirality,

$$
I_8=[\widehat A(T)\operatorname{ch}(E)]_8
=\operatorname{ch}_4(E)-\frac{p_1}{24}\operatorname{ch}_2(E)
+\frac{\dim E}{5760}(7p_1^2-4p_2).
$$

Reversing chirality reverses the entire polynomial. The characteristic-class convention follows the index-theorem formulation reviewed by [Álvarez-Gaumé and Vázquez-Mozo, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.06467v2).

For hand calculations, introduce a degree marker $t$:

$$
\operatorname{ch}_{SU(3)}(t)
=3-c_{2,c}t^2+\frac12c_{3,c}t^3+\frac1{12}c_{2,c}^2t^4+\cdots,
$$

$$
\operatorname{ch}_{SU(2)}(t)
=2-c_{2,L}t^2+\frac1{12}c_{2,L}^2t^4+\cdots.
$$

The character for a bifundamental is

$$
e^{(f_i-f_j)t}\operatorname{ch}_i(t)\operatorname{ch}_j(-t).
$$

For the three one-dimensional stacks, the non-Abelian character is simply 1. Extract its $t^2$ and $t^4$ coefficients and substitute them into the formula for $I_8$.

Here is a compact expression for the sum of all ten sectors. Define

$$U_n=\sum_{i<j}N_iN_j(f_i-f_j)^n,$$

$$
S_c=2(f_c-f_L)^2+(f_c-f_a)^2+(f_c-f_b)^2+(f_c-f_d)^2,
$$

$$
S_L=3(f_c-f_L)^2+(f_L-f_a)^2+(f_L-f_b)^2+(f_L-f_d)^2,
$$

$$C_0=5f_c-2f_L-f_a-f_b-f_d.$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_8={}&\frac{U_4}{24}
-\frac12c_{2,c}S_c-\frac12c_{2,L}S_L
+\frac12C_0c_{3,c}\\
&+\frac5{12}c_{2,c}^2+\frac12c_{2,L}^2+c_{2,c}c_{2,L}\\
&-\frac{p_1U_2}{48}
+\frac{p_1(5c_{2,c}+6c_{2,L})}{24}
+\frac{7p_1^2-4p_2}{240}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The script verifies this formula independently with Cartan weights. It uses color roots $(u,v,-u-v)$ and weak roots $(w,-w)$, then sums the single-weight expression $z^4/24-p_1z^2/48+(7p_1^2-4p_2)/5760$.

### 3.2 Why the four-dimensional calculation still passes

On the closed internal curve, write

$$
f_i^{(6)}=f_i^{(4)}+m_i\omega,\qquad \int_X\omega=1,\qquad \omega^2=0.
$$

For these central fluxes, extracting the coefficient of $\omega$ is

$$
\boxed{\int_X I_8=\sum_i m_i\frac{\partial I_8}{\partial f_i}=I_6.}
$$

The resulting polynomial equals the complete AXG-01 expression,

$$
I_6=\frac{P(f)}6-2A_3(f)c_{2,c}-2A_2(f)c_{2,L}
-\frac{A_{\mathrm{grav}}(f)}{24}p_1.
$$

This is checked sector by sector as well as for the total. The intrinsic spin Dirac index on a curve gives $\deg E$; no extra genus term is inserted. A separate supersymmetric $R$-symmetry twist would require its own background and anomaly terms.

The pushforward loses information. In particular,

$$
C_0(m)=5(0)-2(-3)-3-3-0=0.
$$

Therefore the nonzero six-dimensional term $\frac12C_0c_{3,c}$ contributes no $c_{3,c}$ term to the four-dimensional anomaly. The pure gravitational $p_2$ term also has no internal two-form component in this product background.

**Exact agreement after reduction does not imply that the unreduced parent is anomaly-free.**

## 4. Two parent-level obstructions

The two highlighted terms are

$$
\boxed{I_8\supset\frac12C_0\,c_{3,c}-\frac1{60}p_2.}
$$

The first is the mixed $U(1)$–$SU(3)^3$ anomaly; $c_{3,c}$ is proportional to the cubic color trace. The second is an irreducible gravitational contribution of the specified fermions.

Ordinary six-dimensional two-form Green–Schwarz cancellation uses products $X_4^IX_4^J$, where the invariant four-forms are built from $p_1$, non-Abelian quadratic traces and $f_if_j$. Such products cannot produce the independent $f_i c_{3,c}$ or $p_2$ invariants. This distinction, including generalized cancellation using zero-forms, appears in [Park and Taylor, §§2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5916).

Extra chiral matter can alter the polynomial. Chiral tensors or a gravitational multiplet also alter the gravitational anomaly. They must be counted before testing factorization; adding a tensor counterterm alone is insufficient.

There is a stronger result within a narrow matter class. Let $n_{cL},n_{ca},n_{cb},n_{cd}$ be signed six-dimensional Weyl multiplicities of the color bifundamentals. Their cubic-color coefficient is proportional to

$$
C_{\mathrm{gen}}=
2n_{cL}(f_c-f_L)+n_{ca}(f_c-f_a)
+n_{cb}(f_c-f_b)+n_{cd}(f_c-f_d).
$$

With all five Abelian curvatures independent, $C_{\mathrm{gen}}=0$ requires all four $n$'s to vanish. The quark-doublet index $3n_{cL}$ then vanishes as well.

Thus a parent with only this color-bifundamental matter and only ordinary two-form cancellation cannot retain the desired chiral quarks. Generalized zero-form cancellation, different charged representations, a different gauge realization or additional inflow can change the assumptions. This is not a no-go theorem for every MTFT parent.

## 5. A zero-index chirality choice gives a partial repair

Reverse the six-dimensional chirality of the $(c,\bar d)$ fermion. Its internal index is zero because $m_c=m_d=0$, so

$$I'_8=I_8-2I_8^{(c,\bar d)},\qquad \int_X I'_8=I_6.$$

The mixed color coefficient changes to

$$
C_\Delta=C_0-2(f_c-f_d)
=3f_c-2f_L-f_a-f_b+f_d.
$$

Recall the AXG-01 rows

$$
k_1=(0,-2,1,1,0),\qquad k_2=(3,-4,0,0,1).
$$

Then

$$\boxed{C_\Delta=(k_2-k_1)\cdot f.}$$

It annihilates both $Y$ and $B-L$. The original $C_0$ does not:

$$C_0(Y)=\frac43,\qquad C_0(B-L)=\frac83.$$

Cancelling the original term solely with a native Stückelberg scalar shifting along $C_0$ would therefore give a mass to a combination overlapping the desired hypercharge direction. The chirality change removes that particular misalignment.

A generalized six-dimensional cancellation could use a zero-form $\theta$ shifting along $k_2-k_1$ with an interaction proportional to $\theta c_{3,c}/2$. This addresses the displayed color term locally. The relevant zero-form mechanism is described in [Park and Taylor, §2.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5916).

This is a **partial, nonsupersymmetric diagnostic repair**. The modified fermions still give a $p_2$ coefficient $-1/80$ and other anomaly terms. The operation preserves chiral indices, not necessarily all vector-like modes, masses or interactions. It is also not automatically permitted within a six-dimensional $(1,0)$ supersymmetric spectrum, whose ordinary hypermultiplet fermion chirality is fixed.

The important lesson is that index-zero sectors can be decisive for parent consistency even when they do not change the four-dimensional family count.

## 6. The axion flux-compatibility theorem

### 6.1 A smooth native scalar cannot absorb nonzero net flux

Suppose a periodic scalar $\theta$ exists on the full six-dimensional spacetime, with fixed modulus and shift charge row $\ell$:

$$
D\theta=d\theta+\ell\cdot A,\qquad d(D\theta)=\ell\cdot F.
$$

Although $\theta$ and $A$ can be patchwise fields, their covariant derivative is a globally defined smooth one-form. On a closed internal curve, Stokes' theorem gives

$$
0=\frac1{2\pi}\int_Xd(D\theta)=\ell\cdot m.
$$

Equivalently, $e^{i\theta}$ is a nowhere-zero unit-modulus section of the associated line bundle, so that line bundle must have degree zero.

Hence

$$\boxed{\ell\cdot m=0}$$

is a necessary condition for this smooth native-scalar ansatz. Axion vortices, zeros of a radial partner, boundaries or other sources change the assumptions and require their own action and backreaction. Degree zero also does not by itself guarantee trivial connection holonomy or $D\theta=0$.

### 6.2 Applying the theorem to AXG-01

Direct multiplication gives

$$
\boxed{
Km=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-2&1&1&0\\3&-4&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0\\-3\\3\\3\\0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}12\\12\end{pmatrix}.
}
$$

Consequently, both AXG-01 axions cannot simply be reinterpreted as smooth native six-dimensional scalars with the same shift rows on this flux background.

For any row combination $\ell=u k_1+v k_2$,

$$\ell\cdot m=12(u+v).$$

The compatible subspace is exactly one-dimensional. Its primitive generator is

$$\boxed{k_\Delta=k_2-k_1=(3,-2,-1,-1,1).}$$

An integral basis change isolates it:

$$
U=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det U=-1,
$$

$$
UK=
\begin{pmatrix}3&-2&-1&-1&1\\0&-2&1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad UKm=\begin{pmatrix}0\\12\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is an allowed change of the two periodic axion coordinates. The new first coordinate is $\theta=a^2-a^1$, while the second is $\chi=a^1$.

### 6.3 A more precise candidate to investigate

The parent anomaly and the flux test independently single out $k_\Delta$. This suggests:

| Four-dimensional direction | Candidate higher-dimensional origin | Present status |
|---|---|---|
| $\theta=a^2-a^1$, shift $k_\Delta$ | A native 6D periodic zero-form with generalized anomaly coupling | Passes the degree test; addresses one anomaly term after the chirality change |
| $\chi=a^1$, shift $k_1$ | A mode obtained by integrating a 6D two-form over $X$, or another explicitly derived mechanism | Its tensor sector and induced charges have not been constructed |

A tensor-derived axion is an integral of a higher-form field, not a native circle-valued function on $X$, so the preceding native-scalar proof does not automatically exclude it. This observation is permission to investigate that origin, not proof that it works.

In the new basis the AXG-01 Wess–Zumino coupling becomes

$$a^1X_4^1+a^2X_4^2=\theta X_4^2+\chi(X_4^1+X_4^2).$$

The gravitational Pontryagin term remains in the $\chi$ coupling. No Einstein action or Planck scale follows from this change of basis.

## 7. The scalar potential from an explicit classical ansatz

### 7.1 Dimensional reduction

For a separate vacuum test, assume the six-dimensional bosonic terms

$$
S_6\supset\int\sqrt{-g_6}\left[
\frac{M_6^4}{2}\mathcal R_6
-\frac{h(\sigma)}2(\partial\sigma)^2
-\frac14\sum_i\kappa_i(\sigma)F_i^2-U(\sigma)
\right],
$$

with positive $h,\kappa_i$. The neutral scalar $\sigma$ is not automatically identified with a supersymmetric saxion. Adopt the unwarped Einstein-frame metric ansatz

$$ds_6^2=\rho^{-2}ds_{4,E}^2+\rho^2ds_{X,0}^2,\qquad \rho>0.$$

The measure contributes $\rho^{-2}$; internal scalar curvature contributes another $\rho^{-2}$; internal flux squared contributes $\rho^{-4}$. Therefore

$$\boxed{V_E(\rho,\sigma)=a\rho^{-4}+b(\sigma)\rho^{-6}+u(\sigma)\rho^{-2}.}$$

For reference area $\mathcal A_0=48\pi R_0^2$ and Gaussian curvature $-1/R_0^2$,

$$
a=\frac{M_6^4\mathcal A_0}{R_0^2}=48\pi M_6^4>0,\qquad
u(\sigma)=\mathcal A_0U(\sigma).
$$

For constant-curvature Abelian connections with $\int F_i=2\pi m_i$,

$$b(\sigma)=\frac{2\pi^2}{\mathcal A_0}\sum_i\kappa_i(\sigma)m_i^2>0.$$

In the common stack convention $\kappa_i=2N_i e^{\alpha\sigma}/g_6^2$,

$$
\sum_iN_i m_i^2=36,\qquad
b(\sigma)=\frac{144\pi^2}{g_6^2\mathcal A_0}e^{\alpha\sigma}.
$$

These equations derive the scaling and coefficients from the assumed action. They do not supply $M_6$, $g_6$, $U$, or the function $\kappa$ from arithmetic.

If $u(\sigma)\ge0$, then at every fixed finite $\sigma$,

$$\partial_\rho V_E=-4a\rho^{-5}-6b(\sigma)\rho^{-7}-2u(\sigma)\rho^{-3}<0.$$

Thus even a movable neutral scalar cannot produce a simultaneous stationary point in this class. A scalar extremum cannot compensate for a nonzero radius derivative.

### 7.2 A negative exponential: exact two-field classification

To delimit that conclusion, explicitly assume

$$V=a\rho^{-4}+b e^{\alpha\sigma}\rho^{-6}-c e^{\beta\sigma}\rho^{-2},
\qquad a,b,c>0.$$

The negative bulk term and its exponents are new assumptions. Let $t=\log\rho$ and define positive energy amplitudes

$$A=ae^{-4t},\qquad B=be^{\alpha\sigma-6t},\qquad C=ce^{\beta\sigma-2t}.$$

Stationarity is the two-by-three linear amplitude problem

$$
\begin{pmatrix}-4&-6&2\\0&\alpha&-\beta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A\\B\\C\end{pmatrix}=0.
$$

For $\beta\ne0$, set $r=\alpha/\beta$. Then

$$C=rB,\qquad A=\frac{r-3}{2}B.$$

Because $A,B,C$ are positive,

$$\boxed{\text{a finite stationary point exists precisely when }r>3.}$$

For any positive $a,b,c$, it is unique in $(t,\sigma)$. Indeed its coordinates solve

$$
\begin{pmatrix}-2&\alpha\\2&\beta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}t\\\sigma\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\log\!\left[\dfrac{2a}{b(r-3)}\right]\\
\log\!\left[\dfrac{2ar}{c(r-3)}\right]
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The determinant is $-2(\alpha+\beta)\ne0$ when $r>3$.

At this point the Hessian in the displayed coordinates is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal H=B
\begin{pmatrix}
4(r+3)&-4r\beta\\
-4r\beta&r(r-1)\beta^2
\end{pmatrix},
}
$$

$$\det\mathcal H=4r(r-3)(r+1)\beta^2B^2>0,\qquad
V_*=-\frac{r+1}{2}B<0.$$

The leading principal minor is positive, so this is a strict local minimum in the two tested fields. A positive kinetic metric preserves that stability conclusion, but physical squared masses require canonical normalization. With dynamical Einstein gravity and all other field equations satisfied, the negative energy corresponds to AdS.

Special cases: if $\beta=0$ and $\alpha\ne0$, the scalar derivative cannot vanish. If both exponents vanish, the scalar remains flat. Opposite-sign exponents cannot satisfy $r>3$.

### 7.3 An explicitly artificial positive control

Choose $(a,b,c,\alpha,\beta)=(1,2,8,4,1)$ in arbitrary units. Then $\rho=1,\sigma=0$ is stationary, with

$$
V_*=-5,\qquad
\mathcal H=\begin{pmatrix}56&-32\\-32&24\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det\mathcal H=320.
$$

The coordinate Hessian eigenvalues are $40\pm16\sqrt5$, both positive. This control verifies the classification and its coupled-field stability test. It is not a fit, a derived MTFT vacuum, a physical mass prediction, or a test of all shape and matter moduli.

## 8. Why this still does not calculate the Higgs coefficient

The bare product-group gauge fields are valued in

$$
\mathfrak u(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)^3,
$$

whose dimension is $9+4+3=16$. Their internal components can become four-dimensional scalars, but these remain in the adjoint of each product factor and have zero charges under its centers. They cannot by themselves supply the required charged Higgs doublets

$$q_{H_u}=e_L-e_a,\qquad q_{H_d}=e_L-e_b.$$

The minimal test parent specified fermionic bifundamentals. A fermionic index or a wavefunction overlap does not alone establish the corresponding scalar field and its potential. Added elementary scalars, a composite mechanism, or a larger parent gauge group can change this situation, but each requires its own action. The supplied review response explicitly retains the product group and flags a proposed $U(8)$ embedding as a separate stability problem.

Consequently AXG-01's allowed interaction

$$B_{ij}e^{-ia^1}H_{u,i}\cdot H_{d,j}+\mathrm{h.c.}$$

still lacks a derived coefficient. Anomaly constraints and shift charges cannot choose $B_{ij}$ or the neutral functions $U(\sigma)$ and $\kappa_i(\sigma)$. Changing a gauge-invariant neutral scalar potential leaves the specified fermion anomaly polynomial unchanged; the positive- and negative-bulk controls show that this freedom changes the vacuum conclusion.

## 9. What has changed in the research plan

The next parent construction has sharper requirements than it did after AXG-01:

1. Specify the complete six-dimensional chiral spectrum, including the zero-index sectors and every tensor or gravitational multiplet. Cancel the full $I_8$, not only its flux pushforward.
2. Preserve hypercharge when introducing generalized Stückelberg cancellation. The diagnostic $k_\Delta$ alignment is a concrete candidate, with its nonsupersymmetric limitation explicit.
3. Explain both axion origins. A pair of smooth native scalars with the original $K$ fails the flux test; a mixture of a zero-form and a tensor mode merits a separate construction.
4. Supply actual Higgs scalar representations and interactions, as well as the radius/saxion action. A supersymmetric completion must respect multiplet and twist constraints throughout.
5. Solve the resulting higher-dimensional equations and the full physical scalar Hessian. The two-field AdS control is a test of a mathematical ansatz, not a substitute for those equations.

The four-dimensional arithmetic construction survives this audit as a chiral-index and local-anomaly calculation. The simplest proposed parent does not pass its own consistency tests. The new constraints identify what must change before a scalar potential can be called parent-derived.

All four answers are displayed above. The remaining obstacles require additional physical structure, not an uncomputed identity in the existing charge matrix.
