# Electron–photon experiment plan

7 September 2026. Fixed before running this study's numerical experiments.

The purpose is to test whether the existing X0(143) Hecke operators can constrain a consistent electromagnetic extension. We distinguish exact finite algebra, numerical diagnostics, imported QED structure, and empirical inputs.

1. Correct SM-AUDIT-01 (canonical Higgs quartic) and SM-AUDIT-02 (SU(N) determinant normalization), retain historical behavior, and test the public APIs and a small hot start. Also check invariance of the implemented Wilson and arithmetic actions under local SU(3) transformations. This is an implementation test, not a continuum or mass-gap result.
2. Reuse the previously certified rational projectors onto ell, old, q4, and q6. Test exact projector identities and their commutation with T2. Use the Hodge metric and complex structure to represent the 26 real dimensions as 13 complex dimensions. This conversion does not assign spin or identify particles.
3. Consider the restricted trial charge family Q = sum(q_i P_i). With a neutral mass/mixing operator M, a vectorlike U(1) action requires [Q,M] = 0. Compute the dimension of allowed trial charges for unmixed, old–quartic mixed, and fully mixed cases. Scan 16 fixed seeds (2026090700 through 2026090715), mixing strengths 0, 0.05, 0.2, 0.4, and old–quartic charge differences 0, 0.5, 1, 2. Verify the stripped tree-level Ward identity and finite transformations for representative matrices. Numerical residual gate: 1e-10. These charge differences are diagnostic, not SM assignments.
4. For a conditional electron–photon baseline, explicitly supply 3+1-dimensional spacetime, a Maxwell field, a Dirac spinor with charge -e, and an electron mass anchor. Set e² = 4π alpha using MTFT's existing three-term alpha by default. Include the existing four-term and Monster alternatives without fitting anything. Test a periodic spatial Maxwell discretization at 16, 32, 64, 128 points per direction, with continuous time: two transverse modes, one gauge direction for nonzero momentum, and approach to the assumed continuum speed.
5. Solve the radial nonrelativistic Coulomb problem in Bohr units, with a point nucleus of infinite mass. Use a box of 100 Bohr radii and steps 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, 0.0125; l = 0, 1, 2 and levels through n = 3. Require ground-state relative error below 1e-4 and approximately second-order refinement (ratios between 3 and 5). Repeat the finest grid with box radius 150 to assess boundary effects. Propagate the same alpha to the energy scale. The calculation is not precision hydrogen spectroscopy: recoil, relativistic effects, Lamb shift, nuclear structure, and hyperfine effects are excluded.
6. Evaluate the standard one-loop QED magnetic form-factor integral and allow an additional Pauli coefficient kappa_P = -0.01, 0, 0.01 at fixed mass and charge. Numerically check that its vertex is transverse while its magnetic moment changes. This tests whether the mass/charge data determine a unique magnetic response. No claim of a full electron g-2 prediction or ultraviolet matching is permitted.

Reference snapshots: NIST CODATA 2022 inverse alpha 137.035999177(21), electron rest energy 0.51099895069 MeV. Use experimental uncertainties only to describe reference offsets; the MTFT formulae have no newly derived theory-error distribution. Keep correlated alpha-dependent outputs together, not as independent confirmations.
