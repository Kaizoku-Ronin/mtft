# Dynamics, flat transport, and an explicit Fricke lift

These are exact deductions for the already chosen elliptic family, not a derivation of a preferred dynamics from MTFT.

## 1. Flat interval transport cannot inherit positive exponents just from a chaotic base

Let K be a compact interval in a simply connected region avoiding the singular parameters 0 and -1. Let Y(c) be an invertible fundamental solution of Y'=A(c)Y, where

\[
A(c)=\begin{pmatrix}-1/(4c)&1/[4c(c+1)]\\-1/4&1/(4c)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For any map b:K→K, define each step by Gauss–Manin transport along the interval:

\[
T(c)=Y(b(c))Y(c)^{-1}.
\]

The ordered n-step product telescopes exactly:

\[
T(b^{n-1}(c))\cdots T(c)=Y(b^n(c))Y(c)^{-1}.
\]

Both this matrix and its inverse are uniformly bounded because Y and Y^{-1} are bounded on K. Therefore every singular-value Lyapunov exponent is zero. The same conclusion holds for any continuously varying positive Hodge norm on K, since such norms are uniformly equivalent there. It does not depend on whether b has positive topological entropy or positive tangent Lyapunov exponents.

The hypotheses matter: arbitrary winding choices in a punctured complex base, escape toward singularities, or additional operators are not covered. The interval proof is a coboundary argument, not a statement that every flat bundle has zero Lyapunov spectrum.

## 2. The Fricke parameter map has finite order

For C_c: y²=x⁴+2cx²+c²+c and c≠0,-1, put

\[
w(c)=-\frac{c}{c+1}.
\]

Then w(w(c))=c. Thus its orbit periods are at most two. It is not a period-doubling evolution or a Feigenbaum renormalization operator. Its fixed parameters are 0 and -2; the first is a singular member of the chosen quartic presentation.

The previously verified j-invariant identity only identifies complex isomorphism classes. An actual isogeny between the displayed curve models requires a lift and, generically, an extension of the coefficient field.

## 3. Explicit lift, including the square-root choice

The quartic is birational to

\[
W_c:\quad Y^2=X^3-4cX^2-4cX
\]

by

\[
X=2(y+x^2+c),\qquad Y=2xX.
\]

The inverse on the appropriate affine open is x=Y/(2X), y=X/2-x²-c. Direct substitution proves both equations, and dX/Y=dx/y.

Set c'=w(c) and choose s with s²=2(c+1). Then the following degree-two map goes from W_c to W_{c'}:

\[
X'=\frac{X-4c-4c/X}{s^2},\qquad
Y'=\frac{Y(1+4c/X^2)}{s^3}.
\]

It is the two-isogeny with kernel generated by (0,0), followed by the indicated coordinate scaling. The curve identity can be checked directly after substituting Y²=X³-4cX²-4cX. Over Q(c) the target before scaling is a twist of W_{c'}; the displayed formula is defined after adjoining s. Reversing s changes the lift by the target elliptic involution.

For the quartic de Rham basis

\[
\omega_0=dx/y,\qquad \omega_2=x^2dx/y,
\]

the pullback is

\[
\phi_c^*\begin{pmatrix}\omega_0'\\\omega_2'\end{pmatrix}
=B(c)\begin{pmatrix}\omega_0\\\omega_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B(c)=\begin{pmatrix}s&0\\2c/s&2/s\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Derivation: with Ω=dX/Y, [XΩ]=2[ω₂]+2c[ω₀]. For a general cubic Y²=X³+aX²+bX,

\[
d(Y/X)=\tfrac12(X-b/X)\,\Omega,
\]

so [bΩ/X]=[XΩ]. Here a=b=-4c. Substituting the isogeny formula gives φ*Ω'=sΩ and φ*[X'Ω']=(2[XΩ]-4c[Ω])/s. Converting to the quartic basis gives B.

Choose the reverse square root s' with ss'=2. The two pullbacks compose to

\[
B(c')B(c)=2I.
\]

These choices realize the isogeny and its dual, whose composition is multiplication by two. Choosing ss'=-2 produces -2I instead. After dividing each pullback by √2, the corresponding round trip is I or -I. The determinant-two factor is fixed by the isogeny degree.

## 4. Compatibility with the displayed Gauss–Manin connection

The explicit lift intertwines the connections:

\[
B'(c)+B(c)A(c)=w'(c)A(w(c))B(c).
\]

This is an exact rational identity after writing B=sD, where

\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\c/(c+1)&1/(c+1)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
s'/s=1/[2(c+1)].
\]

Here s' in the last formula means derivative with respect to c, not the reverse square-root choice used above. To avoid that notational collision, equivalently write d(log s)/dc=1/[2(c+1)]. Both sides after dividing by s are

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
(c-1)/[4c(c+1)]&1/[4c(c+1)]\\
1/[2(c+1)^2]&1/[4c(c+1)^2]
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus this is more than a coincidence of j-invariants: with its algebraic lift specified, the isogeny respects the period connection.

## 5. Normalized isogeny pullbacks do not supply exponential Hodge growth

For a finite holomorphic map φ:C→D of degree d between compact smooth curves, harmonic 1-forms satisfy

\[
\|\phi^*v\|_C^2
=\int_C\phi^*v\wedge *_C\phi^*v
=d\int_Dv\wedge *_Dv
=d\|v\|_D^2.
\]

Conformal pullback commutes with the star on 1-forms, and integration of a pulled-back top form multiplies by degree. The equality also holds for complex forms with the corresponding Hermitian norm. Consequently d^{-1/2}φ* is an isometric embedding; for elliptic isogenies it is an isomorphism and an isometry. Every normalized chain of such maps has zero growth in the Hodge norm. Unnormalized chains have the degree-determined factor ∏√d, not a new dynamical universal constant.

The same conclusion applies to the inherited cohomology in the earlier tower C_{m+1}→C_m: its normalized pullback is isometric. The tower creates additional cohomology, but that increase of dimension is a different operation from expanding a fixed transported vector.

## 6. What a nontrivial dynamical model would add

Gauss–Manin transport preserves the symplectic pairing but need not preserve the changing Hodge norm. A nontrivial global cocycle can therefore arise from paths that carry monodromy. A standard projective modular model at level two has parabolic matrices

\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They satisfy (TU)²=-I, while H=TU^{-1} has trace 4 and eigenvalues 2±√3. Repeating this chosen loop word produces exponent log(2+√3) per word. This algebra is exact, but using it as an experiment for our family requires identifying its monodromy basis and prescribing the loop word. A central sign in a lift does not alter the growth. Such deterministic exponential growth is not by itself chaos.

There is an established alternative: add a marked holomorphic differential, normalize its area, and use Teichmüller flow. Gauss–Manin transport then defines the Kontsevich–Zorich cocycle. In genus one the full real H¹ is the tautological plane; its exponents are +1 and -1 with the standard flow g_t=diag(e^t,e^{-t}). Level-two data give a finite modular cover. These exponents depend on the specified time normalization; they are not Feigenbaum constants. Initial differential/direction and the flow are additional data not selected by the triangle involution.

For higher members of the chosen one-parameter hyperelliptic tower, invariance under Teichmüller flow must be proved separately. The known existence of the KZ construction on full moduli space does not prove that this algebraic subfamily, or its inherited/new decomposition, is preserved by that flow.

## 7. A period can inherit Feigenbaum scaling without generating it

At the Feigenbaum accumulation parameter c∞, each fixed-depth member of the earlier tower is smooth. On a sufficiently small simply connected neighborhood, a period P(c) is analytic. Suppose the already selected input sequence obeys

\[
c_n-c_\infty\sim K\delta^{-n},\qquad K\ne0.
\]

If P'(c∞)≠0, Taylor expansion gives P(c_n)-P(c∞)∼P'(c∞)Kδ^{-n}. Therefore

\[
\frac{P(c_{n-1})-P(c_n)}{P(c_n)-P(c_{n+1})}\longrightarrow\delta.
\]

If the first nonzero derivative has order k, the corresponding ratio is δ^k instead. This is the usual invariance of leading scaling under regular changes of observable. Observing it in period data at preselected Feigenbaum parameters would be a consistency check, not an independent derivation of δ or of a renormalization operator on Hodge structures. The cumulative local Gauss–Manin transport from c₀ to c_n converges to the finite transport from c₀ to c∞ whenever the path stays in such a trivializing neighborhood.
