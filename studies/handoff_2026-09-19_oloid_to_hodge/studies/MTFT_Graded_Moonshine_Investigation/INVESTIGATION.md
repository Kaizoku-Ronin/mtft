# MTFT: graded geometry, compatible operators, and the Monster

Investigation against MTFT 0.32.0, 19 September 2026. This is a reproducible research companion; it does not modify the package or establish a physical theory.

The question was whether the Hodge and modular geometry of X0(143) naturally supplies a graded algebra, a symmetry character, and an operator that could support an arithmetic-to-dynamics construction. It does supply an exact graded algebra. The direct Monster identification is obstructed, and two natural lifts of the Hecke operator fail. A different, intrinsic Poisson bracket provides a compatible operation worth developing.

**The graded algebra already exists.** Let X be the compact modular curve X0(143), K its canonical line bundle, and D the sum of the four cusps. Its genus is 13, its modular index is 168, and it has no elliptic points. Thus deg K=24 and deg(K+D)=28. The existing `mtft.canonical` module provides 13 holomorphic differentials, their Fourier expansions through q^140, and 55 quadratic relations. The reported higher generation result is upstream; the new computations below directly verify the dimensions of products through degree five.

The canonical ring is

\[
R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}R_n,
\qquad R_n=H^0(X,K^{\otimes n}).
\]

Its degree-one piece is Hodge's H^(1,0). Higher pieces contain tensor powers of differentials, also called pluricanonical sections. They are not H^(n,0)(X), which vanishes for n>1 on a curve, and not exterior powers of H^(1,0).

Riemann–Roch gives dim R0=1, dim R1=13, and dim Rn=24n−12 for n≥2. Consequently

\[
H_R(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}(\dim R_n)t^n
=\frac{1+11t+11t^2+t^3}{(1-t)^2}.
\]

The degree n counts tensor powers. It is distinct from the Fourier exponent in q=exp(2πiτ). The period coordinate τ, the grading variable t, and a physical time must not be identified without an explicit construction. Riemann–Roch and the modular differential correspondence are described in [Milne's notes](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/MF.pdf), Theorems 1.20 and 4.9.

**Exact symmetry-resolved checks.** The known Atkin–Lehner subgroup is V4, generated by W11 and W13; this investigation does not claim it is the entire automorphism group. Pin the sector order as (++,+−,−+,−−). The shipped degree-one dimensions are (1,6,5,1).

All product ranks in degrees 1–5 were recomputed over both F_1000003 and F_1000033. The q-expansions have enough precision for the weight-2n Sturm bound 28n through n=5. The program checks the original-to-adapted basis transformation exactly, pins fixture hashes, and verifies that elimination pivots occur within these bounds. These are exact finite-field calculations, not floating-point ranks.

Agreement of two primes alone would not prove a rational rank. Here a rank modulo p is a lower bound over Q, while the geometric dimensions give matching upper bounds. The certification remains conditional on the upstream identification of the frozen series as the stated modular forms and on their Atkin–Lehner labels.

The all-degree formulas have a separate geometric derivation. The three involutions have 0, 4, and 20 fixed points. At each fixed point the tangent derivative is −1. Holomorphic Lefschetz gives Tr(W|Rn)=(-1)^n F_W/2 for n≥2; at n=1 the trace is 1−F_W/2. The extra 1 comes from H^1(K). See [Kondyrev–Prikhodko](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06345) for the fixed-point formula.

For ε=(-1)^n, the sector dimensions for n≥2 are

\[
(6n-3+3\epsilon,\;6n-3-3\epsilon,\;
6n-3-2\epsilon,\;6n-3+2\epsilon).
\]

The three exact trace series are

\[
H_{W_{11}}(t)=1+t,\qquad
H_{W_{13}}(t)=\frac{1+t^2}{1+t},\qquad
H_{W_{143}}(t)=\frac{1-8t+t^2}{1+t}.
\]

These provide genuine graded characters for the symmetry already acting on the curve.

**Cusps change the ring.** The full ring of even modular forms is the log-canonical ring

\[
R_{\log}=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}M_{2n}(\Gamma_0(143))
=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}H^0(X,(K+D)^{\otimes n}).
\]

It has dimensions (1,16,44,72,100,128,...) and Hilbert series

\[
H_{\log}(t)=\frac{1+14t+13t^2}{(1-t)^2}.
\]

Ordinary cusp forms satisfy S_2n=H^0(nK+(n−1)D). Thus dim S4=40, whereas dim R2=36. A canonical section in degree n must vanish to order at least n at every cusp when represented as a weight-2n modular form. This distinction is essential to the operator audit.

The log-canonical ring is generated in degree one; [Voight–Zureick-Brown](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.04657), Theorem 4.1.3, covers four or more cusps. Its theoretical AL sectors for n≥1 are obtained by replacing 6n−3 with 7n−3 in the preceding formula. For n≥2, Rlog,n/Rn consists of n copies of the regular representation of V4. This investigation derives those dimensions; it does not construct a full basis of every modular-form space.

**The direct Monster route is closed for these rings.** The canonical ring is generated by its 13-dimensional degree-one space because X is nonhyperelliptic. The log-canonical ring is generated by a 16-dimensional degree-one space. The smallest nontrivial complex representation of the Monster has dimension 196883, as recorded in [Duncan–Griffin–Ono](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6571), equation (2.8). Any Monster action on either generating space is therefore trivial. A grading-preserving algebra action is determined by those generators, so it is trivial on the whole ring.

There is an independent geometric obstruction. A graded algebra action induces an action on Proj R=X, whose automorphism group has order at most 84(g−1)=1008 by Hurwitz. Monster simplicity forces the induced action to be trivial. The remaining fiber scalings are in C*, and Monster perfectness excludes a nontrivial character into C*. A primary reference recalling the bound is [Hidalgo's computational note](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6314).

For comparison, independently expanding E4^3/Δ−744 gives the moonshine conformal-weight dimensions (1,0,196884,21493760,...), already different in degree one. The computational series is q(j−744), so the vacuum is aligned to degree zero. Pulling j back along X0(143)→X(1) is always possible; this does not equip X0(143)'s section ring with Monster states. [Borcherds's proof](https://math.berkeley.edu/~reb/papers/monster/monster.pdf) specifies the much richer vertex algebra used in moonshine.

The obstruction concerns the stated graded section rings. It does not rule out a different construction with additional states or operations. Adding a Monster module by hand would supply symmetry by assumption, however, and would not derive it from this curve.

**Two Hecke lifts fail exactly.** For weight-two forms, the usual good-prime convention is

\[
[q^n](T_2 f)=a_{2n}+2a_{n/2},
\]

where the second term is zero when n is odd. The frozen series through q^140 therefore give exact transformed coefficients through q^70.

| Operation on the polynomial generators | Result on the 55 shipped quadrics |
|---|---|
| Identity substitution | All 55 vanish through q^70 |
| Each W11, W13, W143 substitution | All 55 vanish through q^70 |
| Euler grading derivation | All 55 vanish through q^70 |
| Substitution ei↦T2(ei) | All 55 produce a nonzero coefficient |
| Leibniz extension D_T(ei)=T2(ei) | All 55 produce a nonzero coefficient |

The failure counts refer to this particular basis of relations. A single nonzero exact coefficient is sufficient to disprove preservation of the ideal. For the first (++) relation, substitution gives −37104q²+36820q³+..., while the derivation lift gives −25732q³+96740q⁴+.... Full relation coefficients and smaller witnesses are stored in operator_results.json. Zero controls are supported by the original weight-four Sturm certificate; the negative witnesses require no numerical tolerance.

There is also a small independent example. Set g=η(τ)²η(11τ)²=q−2q²−q³+.... The eta product independently matches (e8+e13)/2. Although T2^(2)g=−2g, one has

\[
[q]T_2^{(4)}(g^2)=1,
\qquad [q](T_2^{(2)}g)^2=0.
\]

Thus the weightwise Hecke operator is not multiplicative. Its weight-four output is not even a section of 2K at this cusp, since its vanishing order is only one. Subtracting a scalar from T2 cannot repair the derivation failure: D_(T2−λI)(Q)=D_T2(Q)−2λQ.

The Serre derivative D2g=θg−E2g/6 has q coefficient 5/6. It is a modular form of weight four but also leaves the compact canonical ring. It does preserve the larger even modular-form ring as a weight-raising derivation.

**A compatible geometric bracket survives.** In a local coordinate z, write s=f(z)(dz)^n and u=g(z)(dz)^m. Define

\[
\{s,u\}=(nfg'-mf'g)(dz)^{n+m+1}.
\]

Terms involving the derivative of a coordinate-change Jacobian cancel, so this is intrinsic. With θ=q d/dq, its modular-form convention is {f,g}=n f θg−m g θf, one half of the first Rankin–Cohen bracket. The overall normalization differs from the z-derivative convention by the fixed scalar 1/(2πi). [Zagier, Modular forms and differential operators](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.1007/BF02830874/fulltext.pdf), formulas (8) and (11a), gives the Jacobi and Leibniz identities.

At any cusp, f=q_c^n A(q_c) and g=q_c^m B(q_c). Their leading derivative terms cancel, leaving a factor q_c^(n+m+1). Hence the bracket belongs to R_(n+m+1), with the required vanishing at every cusp, not merely at infinity. It commutes with pullback by curve automorphisms and therefore respects AL character multiplication.

The bracket is also the ordinary canonical Poisson bracket on the punctured complex cotangent bundle: identify s with f(z)/p^n, where p dz is a nonzero covector. This proves Jacobi globally. It is not a super-bracket merely because its algebraic degree is +1.

The first bracket map is the Wahl/Gaussian map

\[
\bigwedge^2 R_1\longrightarrow R_3.
\]

Thus the 13-dimensional cokernel is exactly the minimum forced by these symmetry sectors. It is a cokernel of this particular map, not a claim of 13 new physical states, dimensions, or particles. The kernel has dimension 78−47=31.

For an explicit nonzero example, let h=e1/72 be the normalized rational newform and g=(e8+e13)/2 the normalized level-11 oldform. Then {g,h}=2q³+... in R3. The reproduction also checks membership in the cubic product spaces and verifies Jacobi and Leibniz on concrete inputs. The finite checks support the implementation; the coordinate and cotangent arguments establish the identities generally.

For h in Rr, the operation D_h(s)={h,s} is a derivation raising degree by r+1. In particular, a degree-one h raises degree by two. This is compatible with the earlier obstruction to nonscalar degree-zero derivations. Formal exponentiation lives in the degree completion; a complete global flow on the uncompleted ring is not established. No real phase space, self-adjoint Hamiltonian, physical time, or quantization is selected by this construction alone.

**A grading ensemble can be defined exactly.** Let N act as multiplication by n on Rn. It is an algebra derivation, and exp(isN) preserves products. Choosing inner products on the finite-dimensional pieces gives a Hilbert direct sum and a self-adjoint number operator. With dimensionless β>0,

\[
Z(\beta)=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta N}=H_R(e^{-\beta}),
\qquad \langle n\rangle=-\partial_\beta\log Z,
\qquad \operatorname{Var}(n)=\partial_\beta^2\log Z.
\]

At t=e^(-β)=1/2, the exact values are Z=75/2, mean degree 241/75, and variance 24794/5625. These are properties of the defined counting ensemble. Assigning energy H=E0 N introduces a scale E0 which this calculation does not determine. The grading phase flow is periodic; it is not a Feigenbaum renormalization map.

**What this establishes and leaves open.** MTFT already includes Hodge-metric quadratic Hamiltonians in `periods/hamiltonian.py` and `surface/dynamics.py`; this study does not claim to introduce its first dynamics. Those operators act on the Hodge vector space. The present question is compatibility with multiplication in the canonical ring, where the bracket offers a separate geometric construction.

No Feigenbaum constant, fine-structure constant, four-dimensional gravitational action, or Monster vertex algebra has been derived. Testing such a connection requires an explicitly chosen evolution or renormalization map, the relevant fixed point and linearization, and a physical interpretation of its variables. The compatible bracket supplies a concrete object for that next investigation; it does not preselect its result.
